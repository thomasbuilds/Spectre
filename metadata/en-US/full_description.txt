Spectre is an Android app for observing and interacting with the wireless environment around you.
It monitors signals from Bluetooth devices, Wi-Fi access points, cellular towers, and GNSS satellites.
Spectre also features a growing set of pentesting tools, constrained by Android's security model.

Features:
• RF exposure - estimated total received power from cellular, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth as one live dBm reading, on a gauge and in the notification
• Cellular - serving and neighbor cells on 5G, 4G, 3G, and 2G, across every active SIM
• Wi-Fi - 2.4, 5, and 6 GHz access points, with security analysis and FTM ranging (802.11mc, plus 802.11az on Android 15+)
• Bluetooth LE - advertising devices with decoded manufacturer data, including iBeacons
• GNSS - multi-constellation, dual-frequency satellite tracking with elevation, azimuth, and range rate
• Local network scan - discover hosts, open ports, and running services on your network
• GATT inspector - connect to a BLE device to read and decode its characteristics (read-only)
• Filter and sort - focus the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth lists on specific devices
• Theme - light, dark, or follow the system

Spectre requests location and other permissions only because Android requires them to read these signals, connect to the devices you inspect, scan your local network, and show the monitoring notification.
Everything runs on-device, with no ads, trackers, analytics, or Google Play Services.
Network access is used only to probe devices on your own local network.
